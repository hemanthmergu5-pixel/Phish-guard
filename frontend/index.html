Understand the threat

What exactly is phishing?

Phishing is a form of social engineering in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person, organization or service in order to influence a victim into taking an unsafe action.

Phishing in simple words

Imagine receiving a message that appears to come from your bank. It says that your account will be blocked unless you click a link and confirm your details.

The message may look convincing. It may contain a logo, professional language and a link that looks familiar. However, the message may actually be controlled by a criminal.

The goal is usually to make you act before you have time to think.

  • ✓ Create urgency or fear
  • ✓ Pretend to be trustworthy
  • ✓ Ask for sensitive information
  • ✓ Send a suspicious link
  • ✓ Manipulate the victim emotionally
1

The attacker creates a story

The criminal creates a believable reason for contacting the victim. It could involve a bank account, delivery, job opportunity, reward, account verification or security problem.

2

The victim receives a message

The message can arrive through email, SMS, social media, messaging applications or even a phone call.

3

The attacker creates pressure

Words such as urgent, immediately, final warning or account blocked can create panic and reduce careful decision-making.

4

The victim is directed somewhere

The attacker may ask the person to click a link, install software, call a number, send money or reveal information.

5

Information or money is targeted

The ultimate objective may be account takeover, identity theft, financial fraud, credential theft or further exploitation.

Common threats

Types of phishing and online fraud

Scams can appear in many different forms. The technology changes, but many scams rely on familiar psychological tricks.

📧

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to appear as if they came from a legitimate organization.

📲

Smishing

Phishing attempts delivered through SMS or messaging platforms.

📞

Vishing

Voice-based scams where attackers pretend to be banks, officials or support agents.

💰

UPI Fraud

Scams involving fake payment requests, collect requests or social engineering.

💼

Job Scams

Fake employment opportunities used to collect personal information or money.

📦

Delivery Scams

Fake courier or parcel messages designed to make victims click links or pay fees.

🎁

Prize Scams

Messages falsely claiming that a person has won money, gifts or rewards.

🏦

Banking Scams

Fraudulent communication pretending to represent a financial institution.

📱

Social Media Scams

Fake profiles, impersonation and fraudulent offers on social networks.

🔳

QR Code Scams

Fraudsters may manipulate users into scanning codes or approving transactions.

📈

Investment Scams

Fake investment opportunities promising unrealistic returns or guaranteed profits.

❤️

Romance Scams

Criminals build fake relationships and eventually request money or information.

Think like a defender

Why do phishing scams work?

Many phishing attacks are not primarily technical attacks. They are attempts to manipulate human decision-making.

⏰

Urgency

Attackers may claim that something must happen immediately. This can prevent a person from carefully verifying the claim.

😨

Fear

Messages about account suspension, legal problems or security threats may intentionally create anxiety.

🎁

Reward

Fake prizes, discounts and financial opportunities can encourage people to ignore warning signs.

👤

Authority

A scammer may pretend to be a bank, manager, police officer, government representative or technical support agent.

❤️

Trust

Attackers may impersonate friends, colleagues or family members to create a sense of familiarity.

🧩

Confusion

Long messages and complicated instructions may distract a victim from noticing the actual request.

Protect yourself

Essential cyber-safety practices

Good security habits reduce the chance of falling for common social engineering attacks.

SAFETY TIP 01

Never share OTPs

Treat one-time passwords as confidential. Never disclose an OTP to someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

SAFETY TIP 02

Protect your PIN

A payment PIN is used to authorize transactions. Keep it private and do not enter it because someone tells you to receive money.

SAFETY TIP 03

Verify links

Carefully examine unexpected links. When possible, access the service using its official application or known website.

SAFETY TIP 04

Use strong passwords

Use unique passwords for important accounts. Avoid reusing the same password across multiple services.

SAFETY TIP 05

Enable MFA

Where available, enable multi-factor authentication to add an additional layer of protection to your accounts.

SAFETY TIP 06

Keep software updated

Operating system, browser and application updates often include security improvements.

SAFETY TIP 07

Don't trust caller ID alone

A phone number displayed on your screen should not automatically be considered proof of identity.

SAFETY TIP 08

Avoid unknown downloads

Do not install applications or files simply because an unexpected caller or message tells you to do so.

SAFETY TIP 09

Check before paying

Before making a payment, verify who you are paying and why the payment is needed.

SAFETY TIP 10

Don't panic

If a message creates intense pressure, pause. Scammers often benefit when victims act quickly without verification.

SAFETY TIP 11

Verify independently

Contact the organization through a trusted method instead of using contact information provided in a suspicious message.

SAFETY TIP 12

Learn continuously

Cybersecurity threats change over time. Continue learning about new fraud patterns and safe digital practices.

If something already happened

Think you may have been scammed?

Do not panic. Take action quickly, preserve evidence and contact the appropriate official organization or financial institution.

🛑

Stop the interaction

Stop communicating with the suspected scammer. Do not provide additional information or send additional money.

🏦

Contact your bank

If a financial transaction or account is involved, contact your bank or payment provider using an official contact method.

🔑

Secure accounts

If you believe your credentials were exposed, change passwords and review account security settings.

📸

Preserve evidence

Keep relevant messages, screenshots, transaction details, phone numbers, emails and other evidence.

In India, cyber-fraud victims can also seek guidance through official government cybercrime reporting channels. Use trusted government resources rather than links supplied by a suspected scammer.
Frequently asked questions

Cybersecurity questions

Quick answers to common phishing awareness questions.

What is phishing? +
Phishing is a social-engineering technique where an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick someone into revealing information, clicking a link, installing software or performing another unsafe action.
Is every suspicious message definitely a scam? +
Not necessarily. A message can contain warning signs without being malicious. PhishGuard's detector is an educational rule-based tool and should not be treated as a definitive security scanner.
Should I share my OTP with bank employees? +
You should treat OTPs as confidential authentication information. Never disclose an OTP simply because someone claims to be from a bank, company or support department.
What should I do when a caller creates urgency? +
Pause. Do not allow the caller's urgency to replace verification. End the interaction if necessary and independently contact the organization through a trusted channel.
Can a scammer fake a company logo? +
Yes. Images, logos, names and professional formatting can be copied easily. Visual appearance alone is not proof that a communication is genuine.
What is smishing? +
Smishing is a term commonly used for phishing attempts delivered through SMS or messaging services.
What is vishing? +
Vishing refers to voice-based social engineering attacks. A scammer may call while pretending to represent a trusted organization.
Why shouldn't I use the link in a suspicious message? +
The link may lead to an imitation website, malicious download or another fraudulent destination. When possible, navigate to important services using a known official application or website instead.
What is social engineering? +
Social engineering is the manipulation of people into performing actions or revealing information that they otherwise might not provide.
Can technology completely stop phishing? +
No single technology can guarantee complete protection. Email filters, browser warnings, authentication systems and security software are useful, but informed users remain an important part of cybersecurity.
